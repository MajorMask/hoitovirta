The problem

The hardest part is the waiting, and nobody owns it.

Three gaps keep the wait long, and longest for people with another language, less income or a distant address.

GAP 1

Nobody owns the waiting

A test result lands with no booked next step. A third visit passes with no test. Nobody is named to notice.

Fast-track and Today: a named coordinator and a list that ranks itself.

GAP 2

Patients wait alone

The letter is in a language they read slowly. They do not know what happens next, or whom to call.

My path: next step, date and nurse in their own language, with a guide that never plays doctor.

GAP 3

Leaders cannot see it

Finland has no figure for the days from first contact to diagnosis, so no county can compare or improve it.

Control Tower and National: the clock, the gaps and a 14-day forecast, with sources.

How it fits

Three ways into cancer care. One clock, from the first contact to the diagnosis.

People arrive through a screening letter, a symptom they notice, or a test ordered for another reason. The official clock begins late. Hoitovirta starts the count at the first contact.

Hoitovirta clock: it counts the days from the first contact to the diagnosis. Nobody measures that today.

How AI is used

AI drafts. A person decides.

AI does

  • Drafts messages in the patient's language
  • Turns report text into fields, with a confidence score
  • Answers patient questions from approved text
  • Ranks the list with a simple score that shows its reason

AI never

  • Diagnoses or rates urgency
  • Starts the clock: a clinician taps
  • Sends anything alone: a person approves

If something fails

  • Emergency words skip the AI and show a fixed message: call 112
  • If no model answers, templates take over and the app still works

Why it can work

It starts with what a county already has.

No integration to begin

An open event standard with a validator, plus CSV and FHIR adapters, lets a county load its own data. One container runs the whole app.

Others have done it

Norway made pathway coordinators and set a 70% target for timely diagnosis. Sweden has a named contact nurse. Denmark's fast pathways cut the diagnostic interval by about 17 days.

Helsedirektoratet · BMC Cancer 2015

Finland has the people

2,223 nurses per 1,000 new cancer cases, against an EU average of 1,376. The missing piece is who owns the waiting.

OECD/EC 2025

A fair first county

Länsi-Uusimaa had among the lowest screening participation in 2024, so progress there would show.

Cancer Registry 2026

Our target: one county, twelve months, about a quarter off the lung-cancer wait, around 45 days. This is our pilot target, not a result: it is half of the 88-day gap to Denmark.

Technology and partners

Built on partner tools, with a fallback for each.

Aiven

PostgreSQL event store

Every step is an append-only event in its own schema. If the database is unreachable, a local SQLite store keeps the demo alive.

Modal

One container, one command

The whole app, the survey and the voice line ship together with modal deploy.

NVIDIA Nemotron on Verda

First in the model chain

Drafts and report fields try Nemotron first, then Gemini and open models, then plain templates.

ElevenLabs

Voice

A voice agent for staff calls and spoken answers for the check-in survey, so a patient can talk instead of tap.

Google Gemini

Fallback model

Takes over when the first model is slow or offline, so a draft still appears.

How it was built

Specs and tests live in the repo. The team made the design decisions and tested the result.

Stack: Python FastAPI · plain JavaScript front end with no build step · event store on Aiven PostgreSQL · deploy on Modal.

Honest limits. A prototype, not a medical device. All data is synthetic and labelled so on every screen. Finnish, Swedish, Arabic and Russian texts need native review; Somali stays behind a human check. Every number and its source.
